♟ Quoridor Rules

7 × 7 board · 5 walls per player · two-player abstract strategy

1 Objective

Each player starts on the middle cell of their starting row. Player 1 starts at the bottom row; Player 2 at the top row.

The goal is to be the first to reach any cell on the opponent's starting row (the far side of the board).

Both players alternate turns. On each turn you either move your pawn or place a wall.

2 Pawn Movement

Move your pawn one cell orthogonally (up, down, left, or right) — no diagonals.

You cannot move through or into a wall segment. Your movement is blocked in any direction where a wall sits between your cell and the adjacent cell.

3 Wall Placement

Instead of moving, you may place one wall. Each player has 5 walls in total. Once placed, walls are permanent.

Walls span exactly 2 cells along one edge. A horizontal wall blocks movement between two rows; a vertical wall blocks movement between two columns.

Critical rule: You may never place a wall that completely blocks a player from any path to the goal row — every player must always have at least one route.

4 Jumping Over Opponents

When the two pawns are adjacent and facing each other, the active player may jump directly over the opponent (if no wall blocks and the landing cell is within bounds).

If a wall or board edge blocks the straight jump, the active player may instead move laterally — one step to either side of the opponent (a diagonal step relative to the jumper's position).

A lateral jump to a given side is also blocked if a wall sits between the opponent's cell and that lateral destination. If both sides are blocked, only the legal ones may be taken.

Diagonal moves are only allowed as these lateral jumps — never in any other situation.

5 Winning & Draws

The first player whose pawn reaches any cell on the opponent's starting row wins immediately.

A draw is declared if the same board position (both pawns + all walls) occurs 3 times, or if the game reaches {{ draw_depth }} total plies without a winner.

Unlike chess, draws are rare — the game strongly favours decisive outcomes because blocking all paths is illegal.

6 This Variant (7 × 7 · 5 walls)

Standard Quoridor is played on a 9 × 9 board with 10 walls per player. This implementation uses a 7 × 7 board and 5 walls per player — a smaller, faster game that is well-suited for self-play training.

All other rules are identical to standard Quoridor.
